How to remove an image background without uploading it

A practical guide to browser-based background removal: what runs locally, what still leaves the device, and how to get a clean transparent PNG.

Advertisement

Traditional online background removers ask you to upload a photo to a remote server. That is convenient, but it also means a copy of your image leaves your device. A browser-native tool takes a different path: the model is downloaded to your browser, and the image never enters an upload request.

What actually runs locally

The page loads a small segmentation model and a WebAssembly runtime. When you pick a file, the browser reads it with the File API, draws it on a canvas, and sends the canvas pixels to the model. The model returns a mask. The page then combines that mask with the original pixels to produce a transparent PNG.

At no point does the tool call an endpoint with your photo as the request body. There is no server-side queue, no object storage bucket, and no database row for the image. The Worker that hosts the site only serves static files.

Steps for a clean result

  1. Use a photo where the subject is clearly separated from the background.
  2. Choose the high-accuracy mode when edge detail matters.
  3. Inspect hair, shoulders, and hands before exporting.
  4. Export a transparent PNG if you plan to place the subject on another color.

Know the limits

Local models are smaller than the large cloud models used by paid studios. They are fast and private, but they may struggle with thin translucent objects, heavy motion blur, or scenes with several people. For profile photos and avatars, they are usually more than enough.

A note about privacy claims

“Your image stays on your device” is accurate. “Nothing leaves your device” is not, because the browser still requests scripts, model files, and possibly advertising resources. A truthful privacy statement separates image data from ordinary web requests.
