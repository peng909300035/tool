How to make a transparent PNG from a portrait photo

A transparent PNG lets you drop a person onto any background. Here's how to cut out a portrait in your browser and export a clean alpha channel.

Advertisement

JPG can't store transparency. If you want to put someone on a colored banner or a new backdrop, you need a PNG with an alpha channel. The usual route is to upload the photo to a background remover and hope the site doesn't keep a copy. There's a quieter way.

What the alpha channel actually does

A PNG has a fourth channel alongside red, green and blue. It tells the viewer how opaque each pixel is. At zero the pixel is fully transparent, at 255 it's solid. Getting a clean cutout is really about getting that channel right around hair and shoulders.

What happens in the browser

Our background remover downloads a small segmentation model to your browser. When you pick a file, the page draws it on a canvas and the model returns a mask, which is just a map of which pixels belong to the person. The page combines that mask with the original pixels and hands you a PNG. The photo itself never goes up to a server.

Getting edges that don't look cut out

Plain backgrounds help more than anything else. A wall or a flat-color studio backdrop gives the model a clear boundary to work with. Busy scenes with chairs, plants and shadows behind the head are where small models struggle.

Check the hairline and the shoulders at full size before you download. If the whole subject disappeared and the background stayed, tick the Invert mask box and run it again. That happens on some images and takes one click to fix.

Export and check on two backgrounds

Download the PNG and open it on a dark background, then a light one. Edges that look fine on white often show a faint halo on black. If you see one, the original photo probably had a light-colored wall behind it that bled into the edge pixels.

Where it falls short

This is a portrait tool. It handles headshots, avatars and profile photos well. It isn't built for products, pets or group shots with overlapping people, and thin translucent things like sheer fabric tend to get clipped. For those cases a manual selection in an editor is still faster than fighting the model.
