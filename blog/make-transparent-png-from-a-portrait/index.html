How to make a transparent PNG from a portrait photo

A transparent PNG lets you drop a person onto any background. Cut out a portrait in your browser and export a clean alpha channel.

Advertisement

JPG can't store transparency. To put someone on a colored banner or a new backdrop you need a PNG with an alpha channel. Most background removers want you to upload the photo first, and then you're trusting them not to keep a copy of it. You can do the cutout on your own machine instead.

What the alpha channel actually does

A PNG has a fourth channel alongside red, green and blue that says how opaque each pixel is. Zero is fully transparent, 255 is solid. Getting a clean cutout mostly comes down to getting that channel right around hair and shoulders.

What happens in the browser

Our background remover downloads a small segmentation model to your browser. Pick a file and the page draws it on a canvas, then the model returns a mask — a map of which pixels belong to the person. The page combines that mask with the original pixels and gives you a PNG. The photo itself never goes up to a server.

Getting edges that don't look cut out

Plain backgrounds help more than anything else. A flat wall or a studio backdrop gives the model a clear boundary to work with. It struggles when there's a chair or a shadow right behind the head.

Before you download, check the hairline and shoulders at full size. On some images the whole subject disappears and the background stays. If that happens, tick the Invert mask box and run it again — one click, sorted.

Export and check on two backgrounds

Download the PNG and open it once on a dark background, once on a light one. Edges that look fine on white often show a faint halo on black. Usually that means the original had a light-colored wall behind the subject, and a little of it bled into the edge pixels.

Where it falls short

It's a portrait tool, made for headshots and profile photos. Products and pets aren't really its job, and overlapping people in a group shot confuse it. Thin, see-through fabric tends to get clipped too. When you hit one of those, a manual selection in an editor is faster than fighting the model.
