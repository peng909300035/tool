How to keep hair detail when removing a background

Why fine edges are difficult for segmentation models and what you can do to preserve natural-looking hair.

Advertisement

Hair is the hardest part of portrait cutouts. Individual strands are thin, semi-transparent, and often overlap the background. A segmentation model must decide whether each pixel belongs to the subject.

Lighting matters more than resolution

A neat, evenly lit photo with a plain wall behind the subject usually produces a better cutout than a high-resolution photo taken against a busy scene.

Look for soft alpha values

A good cutout keeps partial transparency along hair edges instead of a hard on/off mask. The local model uses a confidence mask for this, so individual strands blend more naturally on both light and dark backgrounds.

Check on two backgrounds

Preview the exported PNG on white and black. Hair that looks fine on white may show a gray halo on black. If that happens, try a new source photo rather than repeatedly sharpening the output.

Set realistic expectations

A browser model can produce a usable cutout in seconds, but it is not a manual masking artist. For commercial beauty retouching, a dedicated editor is still the better final step.
