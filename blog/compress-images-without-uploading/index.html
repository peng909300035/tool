How to compress images without uploading them

A practical guide to browser-based image compression: quality settings, format choices, and how to shrink files without a private photo leaving your device.

Advertisement

Image compression reduces file size by removing information your eyes are unlikely to notice. Most online compressors ask you to upload the original file first. A browser-based tool can do the same job locally, with no remote copy of the image.

Lossy versus lossless

JPG and WebP are lossy formats. They discard detail in exchange for smaller files, and the quality slider controls how much is thrown away. PNG is lossless: it keeps every pixel but usually produces much larger files for photographs.

Choosing a quality setting

Start around 80 percent for JPG and WebP. That is often close to visually identical to the original at normal viewing size. Go lower only when file size matters more than fine texture, such as for a preview thumbnail.

A simple workflow

  1. Open a local compressor, for example our image compressor.
  2. Drop in a photo and compare the original and result sizes.
  3. Adjust quality until the file is small enough without visible artifacts.
  4. Download and view it at actual display size before publishing.

Know the limits

Compressing an already-compressed JPG again will not help. Each pass loses more detail. Keep one high-quality master and produce compressed copies from that master instead of chaining exports.

Why local matters

Photos often contain people, documents, or unpublished work. Processing them in the browser avoids creating another copy on a remote server, which is a small habit with a real privacy benefit.
