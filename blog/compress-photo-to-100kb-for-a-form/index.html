How to compress a photo to 100 KB for an online form

Forms often reject photos over 100 KB. Here's how to shrink a JPG to the right size locally, without sending the original to a website.

Advertisement

You've filled in the form, picked your photo, and then it happens: "file too large, maximum 100 KB." A phone photo is usually 2 to 5 MB, so you need to shrink it by a factor of twenty or more.

Why forms set a 100 KB limit

Usually it's storage or email. Some systems attach your upload to a confirmation email, and big attachments bounce. Others cap the database field. Either way the limit is real, and you have to work within it.

Use JPG for photos, not PNG

This alone can save you 80 percent. A PNG of a photograph keeps every pixel and often lands at 8 MB. Save the same image as a JPG and you're down to 1 MB or less before you've touched the quality slider. PNG makes sense for logos and screenshots with flat colors. For anything shot on a camera, use JPG.

The quality setting that works

Start at 65. A typical 3 MB phone photo drops to roughly 150 to 250 KB at that setting, and often below 100 KB at 55. Below 50 you'll start to see blocky patches around edges. Since a form thumbnail is only displayed at a few hundred pixels wide, you probably won't notice.

One thing to watch: compressing a JPG that's already been compressed doesn't help much. Each pass throws away more detail. Work from the original if you still have it.

Steps

  1. Open our image compressor.
  2. Drop in the photo and set quality to 65.
  3. Check the size readout. If it's still over 100 KB, try 55.
  4. Download and upload the compressed copy.

When this won't work

If your photo has lots of small text, like a scanned document or a screenshot, JPG compression turns the letters fuzzy. Keep those as PNG and check whether the form accepts a different format. And if the form insists on 100 KB but also requires a specific pixel size, resize first, then compress. Doing it the other way round gives you a worse result.
