How to compress a photo to 100 KB for an online form

Forms often reject photos over 100 KB. Shrink a JPG to the right size locally, without sending the original to a website.

Advertisement

That "file too large, maximum 100 KB" message is frustrating because a phone photo is usually 2 to 5 MB. You need to shrink it by a factor of twenty or more, and there's no way around it.

Why forms set a 100 KB limit

Usually it's the email. A lot of systems attach your upload to a confirmation message, and large attachments get bounced by the recipient's mail server. Sometimes it's just a small database field. Whatever the reason, the number is hard and you have to work under it.

Use JPG for photos, not PNG

This alone can save you 80 percent. A photo saved as PNG keeps every pixel and often lands around 8 MB. Save the same shot as a JPG and you're at 1 MB or less before you've touched the quality slider. PNG is for logos and screenshots with flat colors; for anything from a camera, use JPG.

The quality setting that works

Start at 65. A typical 3 MB phone photo drops to roughly 150 to 250 KB there, and often below 100 KB at 55. Go under 50 and you'll start to see blocky patches around edges. A form thumbnail only displays a few hundred pixels wide, so most of that won't be visible anyway.

If you still have the original, work from that. Re-compressing a JPG that's already been squeezed doesn't do much, and every pass throws away more detail.

Steps

  1. Open our image compressor.
  2. Drop in the photo and set quality to 65.
  3. Check the size readout. If it's still over 100 KB, try 55.
  4. Download and upload the compressed copy.

When this won't work

Photos with a lot of small text — a scanned document or a screenshot — get fuzzy under JPG compression. Keep those as PNG and check whether the form accepts another format. If the form wants both 100 KB and a specific pixel size, resize first and compress second. The other order just costs you quality for no gain.
